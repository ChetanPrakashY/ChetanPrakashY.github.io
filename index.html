Chetan Prakash

Integrated BS-MS student in Physics, IISER Berhampur, India · Master's thesis at SISSA, Trieste

I am a fifth-year Integrated Bachelor’s–Master’s student in Physics at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Berhampur. I am currently in Trieste, doing my master’s thesis at SISSA with Prof. Enrico Barausse.

I work mostly on gravitational-wave astrophysics, from the waveform models we use to interpret signals from merging black holes to what pulsar timing arrays can tell us about where in the sky a gravitational-wave source sits. I also work on exoplanet atmospheres, turning raw JWST observations into transmission spectra and asking what they reveal about a planet and its star.

Current work

  • Waveform systematics for precessing binaries (Master’s thesis) at SISSA, with Prof. Enrico Barausse and Dr. Rohit S. Chandramouli. How much do different models of spin-precessing black-hole binaries disagree, and when does that disagreement bias what we infer?
  • Source localization with pulsar timing arrays with Prof. Keitaro Takahashi, Kumamoto University. Analytic theory of how well precise pulsar distances can pin down an individual gravitational-wave source on the sky.
  • JWST spectroscopy of WASP-107b with Prof. Vikram Khaire, IIT Tirupati. A panchromatic retrieval of a warm Neptune’s atmosphere that accounts for the spots and faculae on its active host star.
  • Lensing effects in pulsar timing arrays with Dr. Sreekanth Harikumar, Nicolaus Copernicus Astronomical Center and Dr. Apratim Ganguly, IUCAA. Studying lensing effects that can happen on nHz gravitational waves from different lenses and any alteration caused in the PTA Hellings–Downs curve.

Before this, I did research internships at TIFR Mumbai on spinning hyperbolic encounters and gravitational-wave memory events, at IUCAA Pune on lensed neutron-star mergers, and a group project at TIFR on galaxy clustering. Outside research, I am involved in various STEM outreach activities; see activities.
