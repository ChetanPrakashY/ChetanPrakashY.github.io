## The question

K2-18b is a sub-Neptune in the habitable zone of an M dwarf, and one of the most debated planets of recent years. Claims of possible biosignature gases in its JWST spectrum, and the reanalyses that followed, make it an ideal test case for learning how robust exoplanet spectroscopy really is.

## What I did

- **First steps with JWST data:** built white-light and spectroscopic light curves from MIRI LRS time-series products of K2-18b, with polynomial detrending.
- **A full Eureka! reduction** of the MIRI LRS observations, Stages 1–6, from raw data to a transmission spectrum.
- **Compared my spectrum with published reductions** of the same data by Madhusudhan et al. (2025) and Luque et al. (2025).
- **A tutorial notebook:** I turned the reduction into a step-by-step Jupyter notebook from which anyone can learn how to reduce JWST MIRI LRS data with Eureka!, using K2-18b as the worked example. It goes from downloading the raw files from MAST, through all six Eureka! stages, to the transmission spectrum: [K2-18b_MIRI_LRS_Eureka_Tutorial.ipynb](https://github.com/ChetanPrakashY/ChetanPrakashY.github.io/blob/main/assets/files/K2-18b_MIRI_LRS_Eureka_Tutorial.ipynb).

This work was my training ground for the [WASP-107b project](/projects/wasp107b/).
